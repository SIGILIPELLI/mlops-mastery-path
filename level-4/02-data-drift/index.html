    Data Drift: When the Input Distribution Changes¶

    What was run¶

    A real two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, comparing a real reference population (the first 400 rows of the breast cancer dataset, standing in for "what the model was trained on") against two different real "new" batches: one a plain random sample of the same real population, one a genuinely shifted real subset (only large-tumor cases) — checking whether the test correctly tells them apart.

    Real batch A: same population, no real drift¶

    new_batch_same_pop = X.sample(n=100, random_state=1)
stat, p = ks_2samp(reference[feat], new_batch_same_pop[feat])

    mean radius           KS=0.0800  p=0.6680  -> no significant drift
mean texture          KS=0.0550  p=0.9629  -> no significant drift
mean smoothness       KS=0.0925  p=0.4841  -> no significant drift
worst area            KS=0.1025  p=0.3561  -> no significant drift

    Real batch B: a genuinely shifted population¶

    new_batch_shifted = X[X["mean radius"] > X["mean radius"].quantile(0.75)]

    mean radius           KS=0.7250  p=0.0000  -> DRIFT DETECTED  (reference mean=14.32, new mean=19.18)
mean texture          KS=0.3024  p=0.0000  -> DRIFT DETECTED  (reference mean=18.95, new mean=21.54)
mean smoothness       KS=0.1528  p=0.0131  -> DRIFT DETECTED  (reference mean=0.10, new mean=0.10)
worst area            KS=0.6459  p=0.0000  -> DRIFT DETECTED  (reference mean=917.18, new mean=1680.96)

    A real, clean contrast: the same statistical test correctly found nothing unusual in a genuinely unshifted sample, and correctly flagged every one of the four checked features — even mean smoothness, whose real reference and shifted means print identically at 2 decimal places (0.10 vs 0.10) but whose real underlying distributions still differed enough (p=0.0131) for the test to catch what a quick glance at rounded averages would miss entirely.

    How It Actually Works¶

    The KS test compares two real samples' empirical distributions directly — not just their means — by measuring the largest real gap between their cumulative distribution functions. That's exactly why mean smoothness still triggered a real flag despite near-identical rounded means: the two real distributions' shapes differed enough even though their central tendency happened to look similar at low precision, which is exactly the kind of real drift a naive "did the average change much" check would miss.

    Data drift specifically means the real input distribution has shifted — the population of patients, sensors, users, or whatever a model actually sees in production no longer resembles what it was trained on — independent of whether the real relationship between inputs and the correct label has changed at all. That's a distinct real phenomenon from concept drift, covered next, where the inputs can look exactly the same but what they actually mean has shifted instead.

    Common mistakes¶

    • Monitoring only a model's output distribution and assuming that covers input drift too — this lesson's check looked directly at real feature values (mean radius, worst area, ...) arriving at the model, which is necessary to catch a shift before it's even had a chance to affect what the model predicts.
    • Comparing only means or other single summary statistics instead of full distributions — this lesson's real mean smoothness result is a direct, measured counterexample to "the averages look the same, so nothing changed."
    • Running a drift check once and treating the result as permanent — a real reference population needs periodic real re-validation itself, since what counted as "normal" when a model was trained can legitimately evolve over time without that being drift in the production-vs-training sense this lesson measured.

    Exercise¶

    Run this lesson's exact real KS-test methodology against a partial shift — a new batch that's 80% real, unshifted rows and only 20% large-tumor rows — and find the real mixing threshold at which p crosses back above 0.05 for mean radius, to get a direct, measured sense of how much real contamination this specific test can and can't detect at this sample size.
